Income Tax Filing for Startups in India 2024-25: Complete Guide & Deadlines

Introduction

Income tax filing is a critical compliance requirement for startups in India, yet many founders find themselves overwhelmed by the complex procedures and regulations. As we navigate through Financial Year 2024-25, understanding the tax filing process has become more crucial than ever, especially with the increasing scrutiny from the Income Tax Department and the benefits available under the Startup India initiative.

This comprehensive guide will walk you through everything you need to know about income tax filing for startups, including applicable ITR forms, key deadlines, deductions, and best practices to ensure seamless compliance.

Understanding Tax Structure for Indian Startups

Startups in India can be registered under different legal structures, and each has distinct tax implications:

  • Private Limited Company: Taxed as a separate legal entity at corporate tax rates
  • Limited Liability Partnership (LLP): Taxed at 30% plus applicable surcharge and cess
  • Sole Proprietorship/Partnership Firm: Taxed as per individual or partnership tax slabs
  • One Person Company (OPC): Follows corporate tax structure

Most startups opt for Private Limited Company structure due to funding advantages, making corporate tax filing their primary concern.

Which ITR Form Should Your Startup Use?

Selecting the correct Income Tax Return (ITR) form is essential for proper compliance:

ITR-3

Applicable for proprietorship startups and partners in partnership firms who have income from business or profession.

ITR-5

Applicable for LLPs, partnership firms, and Association of Persons (AOPs).

ITR-6

Applicable for companies, including Private Limited Companies and OPCs, except those claiming exemption under Section 11 (charitable trusts).

Most registered startups operating as Private Limited Companies will file ITR-6.

Tax Rates Applicable to Startups in 2024-25

For Companies (including Startups)

Startups registered as companies can choose between two tax regimes:

Default Regime (with exemptions and deductions)

  • 30% corporate tax rate
  • Plus surcharge (7-12% based on income)
  • Plus 4% Health & Education Cess
  • Effective rate: approximately 34.94%

Section 115BAB - Concessional Rate for New Manufacturing Companies

  • 15% corporate tax rate
  • Plus applicable surcharge and cess
  • Effective rate: approximately 17.16%
  • Conditions: Company incorporated after 1st October 2019 and commenced manufacturing by 31st March 2024

Section 115BAA - Concessional Rate for Domestic Companies

  • 22% corporate tax rate
  • Effective rate: approximately 25.17%
  • No exemptions or deductions allowed

Startup India Tax Benefits

Startups recognized by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) enjoy significant tax benefits:

Income Tax Exemption under Section 80-IAC

Eligible startups can claim 100% tax exemption on profits for 3 consecutive years out of the first 10 years of incorporation, subject to conditions:

  • Total turnover should not exceed ₹100 crore in any financial year
  • Must have DPIIT recognition
  • Must obtain certification from Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB)
  • Should be incorporated after 1st April 2016

Exemption from Angel Tax (Section 56(2)(viib))

DPIIT-recognized startups are exempt from angel tax on investments received from residents, eliminating a major compliance burden.

Key Deductions Available to Startups

Understanding available deductions can significantly reduce tax liability:

Section 35(1)(i) - Scientific Research Expenditure

100% deduction on revenue expenditure incurred on scientific research related to business.

Section 35(2AB) - R&D Expenditure

Weighted deduction of 150% for expenditure on in-house R&D facilities (now limited to specific sectors).

Section 32 - Depreciation

Claim depreciation on fixed assets like computers, machinery, and office equipment as per prescribed rates.

Section 37 - Business Expenses

All ordinary and necessary business expenses are deductible, including salaries, rent, marketing, software subscriptions, and professional fees.

Section 43B - Certain Deductions Allowable on Payment Basis

Expenses like GST, employee contributions to PF/ESI are allowed only on actual payment basis.

Common Mistakes Startups Make During Tax Filing

1. Missing the Audit Requirement

If turnover exceeds ₹1 crore (₹10 crore for eligible businesses under presumptive taxation), tax audit under Section 44AB is mandatory. Many startups miss this requirement.

2. Incorrect Classification of Expenses

Confusing capital expenditure with revenue expenditure can lead to incorrect deduction claims and notices from the department.

3. Not Maintaining Proper Documentation

Lack of invoices, agreements, and supporting documents for claimed expenses can result in disallowance during assessment.

4. Ignoring TDS Compliance

Failure to deduct or deposit TDS on payments to contractors, professionals, or rent can attract heavy penalties (up to 100% of TDS amount).

5. Not Reconciling Form 26AS and AIS

The Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS contain critical financial information. Not reconciling these with books can lead to mismatch notices.

Step-by-Step Process for Filing Income Tax Return

Step 1: Gather Financial Documents

Collect all relevant documents including:

  • Audited financial statements (Balance Sheet, P&L)
  • Tax audit report (Form 3CA/3CB and 3CD if applicable)
  • TDS certificates (Form 16A)
  • Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS)
  • Bank statements
  • GST returns
  • Investment proofs for claimed deductions

Step 2: Complete Tax Audit (if applicable)

If your turnover exceeds threshold limits, appoint a Chartered Accountant to conduct tax audit and prepare the audit report.

Step 3: Compute Total Income and Tax Liability

Calculate total income under various heads, apply available deductions, and determine tax liability. Consider which tax regime is more beneficial.

Step 4: Pay Advance Tax or Self-Assessment Tax

If there's any tax liability after adjusting TDS/TCS, pay self-assessment tax before filing the return using Challan 280.

Step 5: File ITR on Income Tax Portal

Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal, select the appropriate ITR form, fill in all details, upload audit report if applicable, and submit. Don't forget to e-verify within 30 days.

Step 6: Verify the Return

E-verify the return using Aadhaar OTP, net banking, or by sending a signed physical copy of ITR-V to CPC Bangalore within 30 days.

Advance Tax Payment Requirements

Companies and businesses (except those opting for presumptive taxation) must pay advance tax if estimated tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year.

Advance Tax Due Dates and Percentages

  • 15th June: 15% of estimated tax
  • 15th September: 45% of estimated tax (cumulative)
  • 15th December: 75% of estimated tax (cumulative)
  • 15th March: 100% of estimated tax

Interest under Section 234B and 234C is levied for delay or default in payment of advance tax.

Penalties for Late Filing or Non-Compliance

Late Filing Penalty (Section 234F)

  • ₹5,000 if return filed after due date but before 31st December
  • ₹10,000 if filed after 31st December
  • ₹1,000 for taxpayers with income up to ₹5 lakh

Interest on Late Payment (Section 234A)

1% per month on unpaid tax from the due date until the date of payment.

Prosecution under Section 276CC

Willful failure to file return can lead to imprisonment from 3 months to 2 years along with fine.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a startup claim loss in ITR?

Yes, startups can report losses in their ITR. Business losses can be carried forward for 8 years and set off against future profits, provided the return is filed within the due date.

Is GST registration mandatory before filing income tax?

No, GST registration and income tax filing are independent compliances. However, if your turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh (₹20 lakh for services in some states), GST registration is mandatory.

What happens if we don't file ITR on time?

Late filing attracts penalty under Section 234F (up to ₹10,000) and interest under Section 234A (1% per month on unpaid tax). You also cannot carry forward certain losses.

Can we revise ITR if mistakes are found?

Yes, you can file a revised return under Section 139(5) before the end of the relevant assessment year or before completion of assessment, whichever is earlier.

Do startups need to pay MAT?

Companies paying tax under Section 115BAA or 115BAB are not subject to Minimum Alternate Tax (MAT). Companies under the regular regime may need to pay MAT at 15% plus surcharge and cess if regular tax is less than MAT.
