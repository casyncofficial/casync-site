Introduction

The Income Tax Department has revolutionized tax compliance in India with the introduction of the Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS), complementing the traditional Form 26AS. For startups, businesses, and individual taxpayers, understanding these three crucial documents is essential for accurate tax filing and avoiding compliance issues.

In this comprehensive guide, we'll explore the differences between Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS, and how Indian taxpayers can leverage these tools for seamless tax compliance in 2024.

What is Form 26AS?

Form 26AS is a consolidated annual tax statement that has been the cornerstone of tax compliance in India for years. It is available to all taxpayers with a PAN (Permanent Account Number) and can be accessed through the Income Tax e-filing portal or your bank's net banking facility.

Key Components of Form 26AS

  • Part A: Details of tax deducted at source (TDS) on salary, interest, commission, and other payments
  • Part A1: Details of tax deducted at source on property transactions
  • Part A2: Details of tax collected at source (TCS)
  • Part B: Details of advance tax and self-assessment tax paid
  • Part C: Details of refunds received during the financial year
  • Part D: Details of paid refunds
  • Part E: Information about TDS defaults or mismatches (Compliance Check)
  • Part F: Details of tax deducted on sale of immovable property
  • Part G: Information related to SFT (Statement of Financial Transactions)

Understanding Annual Information Statement (AIS)

Introduced in November 2021, the Annual Information Statement (AIS) is a comprehensive view of information relating to a taxpayer. It is more detailed than Form 26AS and includes a wider range of financial transactions reported by various sources.

What Information Does AIS Contain?

The AIS provides a 360-degree view of your financial information, including:

  • TDS and TCS details (similar to Form 26AS)
  • Details of specified financial transactions (SFT) such as:
  • Dividend income from shares and mutual funds
  • Interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits, and recurring deposits
  • Securities transactions
  • Mutual fund purchases and redemptions
  • Foreign remittances
  • Purchase and sale of immovable property
  • Cash deposits and withdrawals exceeding specified limits
  • Credit card payments above threshold limits
  • Purchase of shares and mutual funds
  • GST turnover information for businesses
  • Details of off-market transactions

Key Advantages of AIS

AIS offers several benefits over the traditional Form 26AS:

  1. Comprehensive Information: Includes data from more sources beyond just TDS/TCS
  2. Online Feedback Mechanism: Taxpayers can provide feedback if any information is incorrect
  3. Better Accuracy: Helps ensure all income sources are accounted for during ITR filing
  4. Real-time Updates: More frequently updated compared to Form 26AS

What is Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS)?

The Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) is a simplified, user-friendly version of the AIS. It presents the same information as AIS but in a more organized and consumable format that is ready for use in ITR filing.

How is TIS Different from AIS?

While AIS shows raw data from various sources, TIS processes this information and presents it in categories that align with the Income Tax Return format:

  • Systematic Categorization: Information is organized under specific heads of income (salary, house property, capital gains, etc.)
  • Removes Duplicates: Eliminates duplicate entries that may appear in AIS
  • Considers Feedback: Incorporates feedback provided by taxpayers on AIS
  • ITR-Ready Format: Presented in a manner that can be directly used for filing returns

How to Access Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS

Accessing Form 26AS

Method 1: Through Income Tax e-Filing Portal

  1. Visit www.incometax.gov.in
  2. Login using your PAN and password
  3. Go to 'e-File' > 'Income Tax Returns' > 'View Form 26AS'
  4. Download the PDF file

Method 2: Through TRACES Portal

  1. Visit www.tdscpc.gov.in
  2. Register and login with your PAN
  3. Navigate to 'View Tax Credit' or 'Form 26AS'
  4. Download in PDF/text format

Method 3: Through Net Banking

Most banks in India provide Form 26AS through their net banking portals under the 'Tax Credit Statement' section.

Accessing AIS and TIS

  1. Login to the new Income Tax e-filing portal at eportal.incometax.gov.in
  2. Navigate to 'Services' > 'Annual Information Statement (AIS)'
  3. View AIS or switch to TIS using the toggle button
  4. Review the information and provide feedback if needed
  5. Download the statement in PDF or JSON format

Providing Feedback on AIS: A Critical Feature

One of the most significant advantages of AIS is the ability to provide feedback on the information displayed. This is crucial for ensuring accuracy in your tax records.

Types of Feedback You Can Provide

  • Information is Correct: Confirm accurate entries
  • Information is Duplicate: Mark duplicate transactions
  • Information is Incorrect: Flag erroneous data
  • Information Relates to Other PAN: Indicate data wrongly tagged to your PAN
  • Information Is Denied by the Taxpayer: Reject information you believe is wrong

Important Note: Feedback provided on AIS is automatically reflected in TIS, making it ready for ITR filing. However, providing feedback doesn't change the original data; it only marks your response for the Income Tax Department's review.

Best Practices for Indian Startups and Businesses

1. Regular Reconciliation

For startups and businesses, it's essential to reconcile your accounting records with Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS on a regular basis—ideally monthly or quarterly—rather than waiting until the tax filing deadline.

2. Maintain Proper Documentation

Keep comprehensive records of all financial transactions, especially those that appear in AIS, such as:

  • Bank statements showing interest income
  • Property purchase and sale documents
  • Investment statements from mutual funds and securities
  • GST returns and records
  • TDS certificates (Form 16, 16A)

3. Address Mismatches Immediately

If you notice any discrepancies between your records and the information in Form 26AS or AIS:

  • Contact the deductor (employer, bank, or client) immediately
  • Request correction of TDS returns if needed
  • Provide appropriate feedback in AIS
  • Document all communication for future reference

4. Use TIS for ITR Preparation

When preparing Income Tax Returns, rely primarily on TIS as it provides processed information in a format aligned with ITR schedules. However, cross-verify with your own books of accounts.

5. Monitor High-Value Transactions

Be aware that certain transactions are automatically reported to the Income Tax Department through Statement of Financial Transactions (SFT). These include:

  • Cash deposits exceeding ₹10 lakh in a financial year
  • Purchase of shares/mutual funds above ₹1 lakh
  • Credit card bill payments exceeding ₹1 lakh
  • Foreign currency purchases above ₹10 lakh

Common Issues and How to Resolve Them

Issue 1: TDS Credited to Wrong PAN

Solution: Contact the deductor immediately to file a correction statement. You can also provide feedback in AIS marking it as 'Information Relates to Other PAN'.

Issue 2: Duplicate Entries in AIS

Solution: Use the feedback mechanism to mark entries as 'Duplicate'. The TIS will automatically adjust for this.

Issue 3: TDS Deducted But Not Reflected in Form 26AS

Solution: The deductor may not have deposited the TDS or filed the TDS return. Contact them urgently and escalate to the Income Tax Department if not resolved.

Issue 4: Information in AIS That Doesn't Belong to You

Solution: Mark it as 'Information is Incorrect' or 'Information Relates to Other PAN' in the feedback section. Also notify the reporting entity if identified.

Impact on Startup Founders and Key Employees

For startup founders and employees holding ESOPs (Employee Stock Ownership Plans), AIS becomes particularly important as it captures:

  • Details of ESOP exercise and sale transactions
  • Capital gains from securities trading
  • Dividend income from share holdings
  • Angel tax implications on share premium

Founders should ensure that all these transactions are properly accounted for and that any TDS on salary or professional fees is correctly reflected across all three statements.

GST Turnover in AIS: What Businesses Need to Know

A significant addition to AIS is the inclusion of GST turnover information. This data is pulled directly from your GSTN (GST Network) filings and serves as a cross-verification mechanism for the Income Tax Department.

Why This Matters

  • Helps identify discrepancies between GST returns and Income Tax returns
  • Ensures businesses report consistent turnover figures
  • Reduces the scope for tax evasion
  • May trigger scrutiny if significant differences are found

Action Point: Ensure your GST turnover and turnover reported in Income Tax Returns are reconciled and any legitimate differences are properly explained with supporting documentation.
